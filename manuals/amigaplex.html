Supaplex is a fast puzzle game with some action. You play Murphy, a small red ball living inside a computer. In each of 111 levels you must collect enough infotrons and reach the exit, while zonks fall around you, disks explode and snik snaks hunt you through the circuits.

The game was created by Philip Jespersen and Michael Stopp (Think!ware Development, Dream Factory label), with music by David Whittaker, and published by Digital Integration in 1991. It came out for the Amiga and, converted by another team, for DOS PCs.

Amigaplex is the Amiga version of that game, ported to Windows. It is an unofficial fan project. The port does not imitate the game: it runs the original Amiga program, translated instruction by instruction into modern code. The levels, graphics, music and every rule of the game are exactly those of the 1991 Amiga disk; only the parts around the game (window, keyboard, sound output) are new. The package also includes a level editor.

In short: unpack the archive, start amigaplex.exe, click anywhere to skip the intro, click new player, type your name, press Enter, then Space to start level 1. Move with the arrow keys.

Getting started

What's in the package

Amigaplex\
  amigaplex.exe          the game
  amigaplex_editor.exe   the level editor
  SDL2.dll              library used by both programs (keep it next to them)
  Amigaplex_Manual.html  this manual
  data\                 the game itself: levels, graphics, music (from the Amiga disk)

The game keeps everything in its own folder. Nothing is installed and nothing is written anywhere else, so you can copy the folder to a USB stick and play on any Windows PC. When you play for the first time, two files appear next to the game:

File What it holds
hiscores.sav the players, their progress and the Hall of Fame
amigaplex.ini display settings (see Display and settings)

Requirements

Any 64-bit Windows 7 or newer. A gamepad is optional.

Controls

The Amiga game was played with a joystick (in the level) and a mouse (in the menu). The port lets you use the keyboard or a gamepad instead of the joystick.

Where Action Keys and buttons
Intro skip to the main menu any key or mouse click
Main menu press buttons, pick players and levels mouse
start the selected level Space, the OK button, or the gamepad's fire button
Typing a player name finish / erase Enter / Backspace
In a level move Murphy arrow keys, gamepad D-pad or left stick
fire (snap, plant a red disk) Space, gamepad A / B / X / Y
pause / continue P or the right mouse button
give up (Murphy explodes) Esc (the left mouse button does nothing in a level, so a stray click cannot blow Murphy up)
"Click to continue" screens continue click, Space, Enter or Esc
Anywhere full screen on / off F11 or Alt+Enter
freeze everything (a pause the game doesn't know about) Pause
quit F12 or close the window

The mouse is not captured: the Windows cursor moves the game's pointer and can leave the window at any time. When the window is minimized, the game and the music simply wait until you come back.

How to play

The goal

Every level is a board of 60 × 24 squares, larger than the screen, which scrolls as Murphy moves. The counter at the bottom right shows how many infotrons you still need. When it reaches zero, walk into the exit. If Murphy is caught by an enemy, crushed or blown up, the level is lost: you are back in the menu and can try again as often as you like.

The panel under the board shows the player, the time spent on this level, the level number and name, and the infotrons still needed.

The main menu

Part of the menu What it does

delete player remove the selected player
skip level give up on the current level and go to the next one; at most three levels can be skipped at a time, and you will have to solve them later

gfx-tutor "The Cast": a picture of every object with its name

LEVEL LIST arrows choose the level; you can only play levels you have already reached (the ones not reached yet are shown in red)
OK start the selected level (as Space does)

the little red button right of the message line the credits screen
the "1 ⇄ 2" disk button on the Amiga: load another data disk (in the port it reloads the same data)

A few names are refused when you create a player. Try them.

The cast

Object What it does
Murphy you
Infotron collect it by walking into it. Infotrons fall and roll like zonks, and a falling infotron kills Murphy too
Exit open when enough infotrons are collected
Base circuit board. Murphy eats it as he walks, and nothing else can pass through it
Zonk a heavy ball. It falls when there is nothing under it and rolls off zonks, infotrons and RAM chips. Murphy can push a zonk left or right (lean on it for a moment) if the square behind it is empty. A falling zonk kills Murphy and crushes enemies
RAM chip a wall; zonks and infotrons roll off it
Hardware a solid wall that even explosions cannot destroy
Ports Murphy passes through a port in the direction of its arrows (one-way, two-way, four-way), if the square behind it is free. Some ports look normal but are special ports: they switch gravity and freeze zonks or enemies
Snik snak a pair of scissors that follows the walls. Touching it is deadly. Drop a zonk or an infotron on it, or blow it up
Electron like a snik snak, but when it is destroyed its explosion turns into infotrons (up to nine)
Bug a base that sparks from time to time. Do not step on it while it sparks
Orange disk (utility 1) explodes when it falls onto anything or when something falls on it. Push it left or right
Red disk (utility 2) a bomb to pick up. Collected red disks can be planted anywhere
Yellow disk (utility 3) can be pushed in all four directions. All yellow disks explode when Murphy touches a terminal
Terminal touch it to set off every yellow disk in the level

An explosion destroys everything in a 3 × 3 square around it except hardware; orange disks and enemies caught in it explode too. Murphy dies if he is in it.

Moves worth knowing

  • Snapping. Hold fire and press a direction: Murphy eats the base, takes the infotron or picks up the red disk next to him without moving. This way you can clear the square under a zonk without standing under it.
  • Pushing. Keep pressing towards a zonk or a disk for a moment before it moves.
  • Planting a red disk. Stand still and hold fire (without a direction) for about half a second. The disk explodes about 0.7 seconds later: run!
  • Gravity. In some levels (or after a special port) Murphy falls like a zonk. He can still climb through ports and hold on to bases.
  • Pause and think. Supaplex has no time limit. P dims the screen and stops the game; the clock of the level stops too.

Amiga and DOS: what's different

Supaplex is best known in its DOS version. The level files have the same layout, and most levels play alike, but the two versions are separate programs, written by different people: the DOS conversion was programmed by Robin Heydon with graphics by Matt Smith. The comparison below was made against the DOS game's own code (as reconstructed by the OpenSupaplex project from the disassembled DOS version with SpeedFix 6.3, the one most players use today). Leaving aside the finer points of movement and timing, these are the differences you will notice:

Amiga (this port) DOS
Sound the music plays all the time; there are only two sound effects: explosions and walking into the exit music (AdLib, Sound Blaster, Roland) that can be switched off, and effects for exploding, collecting an infotron, pushing, falling, eating base, a sparking bug and the exit
Controls joystick in the level, mouse in the menu, keyboard only for names (the port adds keys, see below) keyboard (arrows, Space, P pause, Esc give up), joystick optional
Edge of the board Murphy stops at the edge, even where the frame of hardware has a gap. But if the level's start screen looks outside the board (for example, a level made with a DOS editor, which writes zeros there), the stop no longer works and Murphy can walk off the board no stop at all. Horizontally Murphy comes out on the opposite side, one row higher or lower (the board is one long strip of squares). Vertically nothing wraps: past the top or bottom row he walks into memory outside the level
Start screen of a level stored in each level (4 bytes that DOS leaves unused): the view starts where the level designer wanted always centred on Murphy
Zonk settings 0 normal; 1 "no crush", Amiga only: zonks fall but do not crush enemies, and survive the explosion of what they land on; 2 frozen. With 1 and 2, infotrons do not crush enemies either 2 frozen, anything else normal; falling infotrons always crush enemies
Special ports the settings switch as soon as Murphy tries to enter the port, even if he cannot pass; the game looks at every entry, ignoring the "number of ports" byte the settings switch after Murphy has passed; only the number of entries given in the level counts
Planting a red disk hold fire (without a direction) for about 0.6 s; the disk explodes about 0.7 s later a short press of fire while standing still; the disk explodes about 1 second later
Menu new player, delete player, skip level, statistics, gfx-tutor, demo; the floppy button loads another data disk the same, plus Controls (sound card, music and effects on/off, keyboard or joystick)

Level files made for DOS can be imported into the editor (see below), which adapts them to the Amiga rules.

What the port adds

Everything below is about how you control the game and how it looks. The game logic is not changed: every level plays exactly as on an Amiga.

  • No Amiga needed, no disk image. The data files are already in the data folder.
  • Keyboard and gamepad. Arrow keys and Space work as the joystick inside a level; any gamepad works everywhere.
  • Keys as in the DOS version: Space in the menu starts the level, Esc gives up, P pauses, and Space, Enter or Esc continue from the results, credits and statistics screens.
  • Skippable intro: any key or click jumps straight to the menu.
  • No accidental clicks. On the Amiga a level took a few seconds to load from floppy, so the button you clicked OK with was already released when the level started. Here loading is instant, so the port waits until you let go. Without this, the click on a results screen would press a button in the menu underneath. Space works the same way: holding it from the previous screen does not start a level.
  • No stray give-ups. On the Amiga the left mouse button blows Murphy up (gives up the level), so one careless click ends the game. Here the left button does nothing in a level or the demo: give up (or stop the demo) with Esc, as in the DOS version. A press that started in a level does not click on the results screen either.
  • Smooth scrolling. Vertical sync, and on request a 50 Hz full-screen mode for perfectly even motion (see below).
  • Pause on minimize, a window that can be resized, full screen with F11.
  • Level names in the menu follow your edits. On the Amiga disk the names shown in the menu's level list are stored separately from the levels. The editor updates both.
  • Credits. The credits screen has a new line about the port.

Display and settings

The Amiga shows 50 pictures per second. Most PC monitors run at 60 Hz, where 50 pictures can't be spaced perfectly evenly: every fifth one stays on screen a little longer. Three ways to deal with it are in amigaplex.ini (open it with Notepad; it is created on the first start):

Setting Meaning
scale=3 window size: the Amiga picture × 3
fullscreen=0 1 starts in full screen
vsync=1 draw in step with the monitor: no tearing
fullscreen_50hz=1 in full screen, switch the monitor to 50 Hz (or 100 Hz) if it supports it: perfectly smooth at the true speed
speed=amiga display runs one game step per monitor refresh: always smooth, but on a 60 Hz monitor the game is 20% faster than on the Amiga

Command line

amigaplex [--scale N] [--fullscreen] [--reset-hiscores] [--original-hiscores]
          [--data FOLDER] [--save FILE]
  • --reset-hiscores: start over with no players.
  • --original-hiscores --reset-hiscores: restore the players and the Hall of Fame from the original disk.
  • --data, --save: use another data folder or save file (for example, to keep a separate set of levels).

The level editor

amigaplex_editor.exe edits the 111 levels in data\LEVELS.DAT. It draws them with the game's own graphics, so what you see is what you get.

  • Drawing. The left button draws with the main tile and the right button with the second one (empty by default). Tools: pencil, line, frame, box, fill, select. Alt+click or the middle button picks a tile from the map.
  • Selections. Copy, cut and paste as many times as you like, mirror while pasting (X / Y). Ports turn around when mirrored, and special port settings travel with their ports.
  • Unlimited undo (Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y) for each level.
  • Level settings under the map: title, zonks (normal, no crush, frozen), infotrons needed (0 = all), gravity, and the start camera: the part of the level the game shows first. It is set automatically by default. To change a number, click it and type a new one, or hold − / +.
  • Special ports are shown in blue. Each has its own gravity and freeze settings (at most 10 per level).
  • Check (F7) lists problems: no Murphy, no exit, too few infotrons, gaps in the border, a camera outside the board, and so on. Click a line to see the spot.
  • Play (F5) starts the game directly on the level you are editing, even before saving. Your own players and scores are not touched. Press Esc to return.
  • Import and export. Drop a .SP level file or a whole LEVELS.DAT (also from the DOS version) onto the window. Ctrl+E exports the level as .SP, Ctrl+P saves a picture of it.
  • Compact mode (Tab) hides the side and bottom panels; on a Full HD screen the whole level then fits at 32 pixels per square.
  • English or Russian (EN / RU), light or dark theme (☀ / ☾), full screen with F11 or Alt+Enter. The editor remembers its settings and its window; on start the whole level is fitted into the window. F1 shows every shortcut.

The editor also follows the Amiga's rules by itself, so levels from DOS editors work properly: it sets the start camera, keeps the special ports in order and warns about gaps in the border (on the Amiga, a zonk or an enemy that leaves the board hangs the game). Levels you have not touched are saved byte for byte. Before the first save, the old files are kept as LEVELS.DAT.bak and GRAPHICS.BIN.bak.

Questions and answers

Where are my saves? In hiscores.sav next to the game. Copy the whole folder and your players come along.

The game says "COLORBLIND I GUESS...". That level isn't open yet: the red ones in the level list can't be played until you reach them.

The picture judders a little on my monitor. See Display and settings: try fullscreen_50hz=1 or speed=display.

Why are there so few sound effects? That's how the Amiga version sounds: it has music plus two effects. Nothing is missing.

Can I play my own levels in the game? Yes: save them in the editor (they go into data\LEVELS.DAT) and start the game. Keep a copy of the original data folder if you want to go back.
